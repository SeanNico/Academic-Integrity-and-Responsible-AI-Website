Types of Intellectual Property

Learn Types of Intellectual Property.

Patient

A special right granted to an innovator for their own product, machinery, or methodology. After a patent is granted, only the patent holder possesses the authority to produce their own innovation, and nobody else. In general, patents remain in effect for up to 20 years.



Example

Dyson invented and patented the technology that is used in bagless vacuum cleaners. The patent they filed for legally safeguards their invention and prohibits others from using their technology commercially without authorization.

Trademark

A word, symbol, or phrase that identifies the product or service being marketed by a particular business or company. Trademarks serve to differentiate similar products that are circulating in the marketplace.

  • Ø “TM” - Unregistered trademark used in goods.
  • Ø “SM” - Unregistered trademark used with services.
  • Ø “®” - Registered trademark used with goods or services.


Example

Puma's Puma Logo. Puma relies on its well-known Puma logo symbol to distinguish its products and establish its identity in the market. Trademarks safeguard the brand symbol and help ensure it is recognized as authentic.

Copyright

Copyright safeguards original works of authorship. This form of intellectual property safeguards a wide variety of creative works, such as paintings, musical compositions, illustrations, photographs, and such comparable works. A copyright is meant to protect the creative expressions underlying the work rather than ideas, methods, concepts, or discoveries.



Example

Neon Genesis Evangelion. Yoshiyuki Sadamoto's books are protected under copyright regulations. Original written stories are shielded by copyright. Without permission, individuals are not allowed to reproduce or distribute copies legally.

Trade Secrets

Trade secrets are a form of Intellectual Property that provides a company with an advantage over the enormous sea of competitors, including formulas, manufacturing methods, client lists, advertising strategies, and more. In contrast to Copyrights, Trademarks, and Patents, no federal office or agency is responsible for registering this kind of Intellectual property.



Example

Coca-Cola keeps its beverage formula secret by treating it as a trade secret. Trade secrets are secured within a company; thus, they are hidden from competitors, an advantage that gives the company a competitive advantage.

KEYPOINTS

Key Point

  • Each unique piece or innovative work can be called intellectual property.
  • Literature, Drama, Music, and Artistic works.
  • There are also intangible innovations, formulas, business methods, logos, taglines, color schemes, and website content, which are among the wide variety of intangible assets that fall under intellectual property law.
  • Patents - Protects inventions.
  • Trademarks - Protects brand names and logos.
  • Copyright - Protects creative works.
  • Trade Secret - Protects confidential business information.
